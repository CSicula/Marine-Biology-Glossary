# Big Marine Biology Glossary



### Cell biology:





* \[p]
* ABC model / ABCDE model - Models for the gene expression in the whorls of structures within flowers.
* Actomyosin - A complex of actin and myosin that generates force and movement in cells.
* Apical meristem - Source of new growth in plants, divided into the Shoot apical meristem (SAM) and Root apical meristem (RAM).
* Autonomous (flowering pathway) - When flowering is triggered without stimulus, after a long period without flowering.
* Auxin - A phytohormone with a variety of functions including phototropism and controlling root/shoot branching. Antagonistic to Cytokinin.
* Blastocoel - A fluid-filled cavity within the blastula.
* Blastula - A stage in development consisting of a hollow sphere of cells (blastomeres) surrounding a blastocoel.
* BMP - A growth factor protein signalled in the ectoderm that promotes epidermal covering.
* Brassinosteroids - Phytohormones responsible for a variety of functions including seed germination and stress tolerance.
* Canalization - The ability to consistently express a phenotype without change to said phenotype.
* Casparian strip - A structure in the endodermis composed of lignin, restricts the flow of water in the apoplast pathway (through cell walls).
* Cell fate - The determination of what kind of cells undifferentiated or stem cells will differentiate into.
* CO - Constants; A transcription factor in the photoperiodic pathway that ensures FT production is stimulated only when daylight hours are long enough (summer).
* Columella - The outermost layer at the base of the RAM allowing easier passage through soil.
* Cortex - The main region of tissues in plant roots, providing structure and standard metabolic processes
* Cortical Tractor Model - A model of how the epithelium of the embryo folds inward during morphogenesis, involving actomyosin.
* Cytokinin - A phytohormone with a variety of functions including stem cell maintenance and controlling root/shoot branching. Antagonistic to Auxin.
* Diploblastic - Gastrulation consisting of a gastrula with only two layers (the endoderm and ectoderm).
* Discoidal - One of the forms of meroblastic cleavage which division is restricted to a disc of cytoplasm on the surface of the zygote.
* E/C initial - A group of specialised meristem cells in the RAM responsible for differentiating into the cortex and endodermis as the root grows.
* Ectoderm - The outermost germ layer in a developing embryo.
* Endoderm -The innermost germ layer in a developing embryo.
* Endodermis - A region of tissue in the root responsible for regulating the movement of molecules in/out of the vascular system. Contains the Casparian strip.
* Epigenesis - The process of organisms developing from a seed/egg by gradual differentiation.
* FLC - A phytohormone that inhibits FT, therefore preventing the growth of flowers.
* Florigen (FT) - A phytohormone in the hormone pathway that results in flowering by inducing meristem tissue to switch from a vegetative to reproductive identity.
* Gastrodermis - The cells acting as the inner membrane lining of the gastrovascular cavity (digestive system) in cnidarians.
* Gastrulation / Gastrula - A stage in early development where a two or three-layered gastrula is formed, developed from the blastula.
* Genetic patterning - The process by which the spatial arrangement of cell fates are developed.
* Germ layers - Layers of cells present in gastrulation: the Ectoderm, Mesoderm or the Endoderm.
* Gibberellin - Phytohormone responsible for a variety of functions including growth, flowering and fruit development.
* Gravitropism - The growth of a plant with respect to gravity i.e. the upward growth of shoots and downward growth of roots.
* Holoblastic - Division process in early development by which the entire zygote divides.
* Homeotic - A biological process mediated by hormones/homeostasis.
* Hox genes - Genes responsible for determining the posterior and anterior (back and front) ends of an animal. Absent in ctenophores and sponges and reduced in cnidarians.
* Inductive interactions - Interactions between two cells in which information/signals are passed between them.
* Lateral root cap - An outer layer of the sides of the RAM, assists with passage through the soil.
* MADS box genes - A group term of transcription factors that includes any homeotic genes in plants, responsible for functions such as development and flowering.
* Meroblastic - Division process in early development by which only part of the zygote divides.
* Mesoderm - The central/middle germ layer in a developing embryo.
* Morphogenesis - The stage of embryonic development where the physical form of the organism arises.
* Morphogens - A group of signalling molecules involved with cell fates in morphogenesis. Includes Shh and Wnt.
* Neural groove - A shallow groove along the neural plate of an embryo.
* Neural plate - A thickened plate of the ectoderm in embryos.
* Notochord - A chord running through the mesoderm of embryos, acts as a signalling centre and later develops into the spinal nerve chord.
* Organ primordia - Early cell clusters in embryos that give rise to organs.
* Organogenesis - A stage of embryonic development where the germ layers develop into tissues and organise into the organs.
* Parasegments - A distinct division expressed in segmentation in a developing embryo.
* Pax6 - A transcription factor that is extremely important in the development of eyes.
* Photoperiodic - A flowering pathway that initiates flowering in a circadian rhythm with respect to day length.
* Phototropism - The growth of a plant with respect to the direction of light.
* Phyllotaxis - Optimisation in growing e.g. plants growing wide leaves to maximise sunlight exposure.
* Phytohormones - Proteins in plants acting as chemical messengers.
* Plasticity - The ability to express a phenotype that is flexible and can adapt to the environment.
* Proteolysis - The breaking down of proteins into smaller polypeptides and proteins.
* Protoderm - the outermost meristem tissue of the apical meristem (consisting of the epidermis and root hairs); the main source of new growth. Also refers to the growing regions of plant embryos.
* Quiescent centre - A group of specialised meristem cells in the RAM responsible for the maintenance of the meristem tissue in the RAM.
* Scarecrow (SCR) - A transcription factor assisting with the differentiation of meristem cells into the endodermis and cortex.
* Second messenger - Signalling molecules that carry and amplify information in plants.
* Shortroot (SHR) - A transcription factor responsible for the differentiation of meristem cells into the endodermis. It also induces the expression of SCR.
* Sonic Hedgehog (Shh) - A morphogen contributing to limb development and branching.
* Somite - A group of cells in the mesoderm of embryos that later develops into skeletal and muscular tissue.
* Sox2 - An important transcription factor with various contributions to embryonic development.
* Strigolactones - Phytohormones responsible for attracting symbiotic mycorrhizal fungi.
* Superficial - One of the forms of meroblastic cleavage in which nuclei divide in a shared region in the cytoplasm before migrating out to the surface, where cells are formed.
* Totipotency - How (plants?) deal with physical damage and stress.
* Transcription factors - Proteins responsible for switching on and off genes.
* Triploblastic - Gastrulation consisting of a gastrula with three layers (the endoderm, mesoderm and ectoderm).





##### Cell biology - less important specifics



* \[p]
* Actin - A protein involved with muscle contraction. 
* EMB30 - A plant mutant involved with cell cleavage in plant zygotes.
* FASS - A plant mutant involved with cell cleavage in plant zygotes.
* FGF - Growth factor signalling proteins with a variety of applications.
* GI - A phytohormone in the photoperiodic pathway that results in flowering.
* LFY - A phytohormone in the hormone pathway that results in flowering.
* Myosin - A protein involved with muscle contraction.
* ROS - Relative Oxygen Species; an important second messenger in plants, initial cytotoxic as it breaks disulphide bonds.
* SOC1 - A phytohormone in the hormone pathway that results in flowering.
* Wnt - Signalling proteins with broad applications for transferring information between cells.
